## Owner rules (highest priority — written by the site owner, never removed by agents)

<!-- add owner rules here, e.g. "- Always prefer US cost/pricing keywords for the SaaS silo." -->
- 2026-10-10 (escalated by onpage-seo-auditor, repeat gap, not an owner rule; owner may edit): every keyword the brief hands over that is not a plain noun phrase needs a carrier pattern. That covers the primary keyword, every `secondary` and `ngrams` entry that holds "vs", and reversed-order city keywords. Evidence: 10-07 primary "ux audit agency vs freelancer" (no carrier), 10-09/10-10 city keywords stacked on Philadelphia, Chicago and Columbus, and 10-10 secondary "discovery phase vs mvp". The last had no carrier, so the post shipped "so discovery phase vs MVP is a false choice". `keywordCarrier.inBody` covered only the primary. Rule: add `keywordCarrier.secondary` with one sentence pattern per such entry, or drop the entry. Good: `"discovery phase vs mvp": "framing it as discovery phase vs MVP sets up a false choice"`. Bad: listing "discovery phase vs mvp" in `secondary` with no pattern. Update 2026-10-11 (repeat, cause in the brief): the mobile brief's `keywordCarrier.inBody` listed "weighing a mobile app design agency vs freelancer" as an approved carrier. The writer used it word for word in the closing, and the auditor had to fix it to "against a freelancer". A carrier must be a full grammatical phrase on its own: either the pair plus a head noun ("the X vs Y question/decision") or an article on both halves. Read each carrier aloud as "versus" before you put it in the brief. Good: "the mobile app design agency vs freelancer question". Bad: "weighing a mobile app design agency vs freelancer".
- 2026-10-12 (owner): pick only keywords with verified search volume above 0 (Semrush keyword overview, US). Record `volume`, `kd` and `dataSource` with the date in the brief; never estimate. If Semrush returns no_api_units, use Google Search Console impressions for the exact query as evidence (figure + date range). If neither gives a number, do not hand over a brief: return "BLOCKED: no volume data" so the orchestrator stops and tells the owner. Every brief must also list `entities` (15–25) and `attributes` (8–12) for the writer; the on-page script scores coverage against them.

## Lessons (learned from runs, QA, audits and results)

<!-- lessons below -->

- 2026-10-05 (ux-research-services, first BOFU). Evidence: the hub JSON has its own H2 "How much does UX research cost?" with a price range, so "ux research cost" would cannibalise it even though the hub's metaTitle does not say "cost". Rule: check the hub's `sections[].h2` and FAQ questions, not only `keyword`/`metaTitle`. If a hub H2 already answers the query, reject it as a post keyword and use a buyer-evaluation angle (how to choose, compare proposals) that links up to the hub's section.
- 2026-10-05. Evidence: Semrush returned `no_api_units` on the first call and WebFetch was blocked by the egress proxy for every agency blog tried (fuselabcreative, ux4sight, answerlab, 925studios, eleken). Rule: test Semrush with one call before planning around it; when WebFetch is blocked, use `<domain> <post title>` WebSearch queries to get each page's coverage from snippets, set `volume`/`kd` to null, and state the data source and its limits in the brief's `dataSource` field. Never estimate volume or KD.
- 2026-10-05. Evidence: SERP snippets for agency-hiring queries carried unsourced numbers ("3x more likely to report satisfaction"). Rule: list such claims in the brief `notes` as "do not use" so the writer does not pick them up from the competitor page.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-ux-design, first BOFU). Evidence: the obvious BOFU query "how to choose a SaaS UX design agency" had a weak, beatable SERP, but it would repeat the format of the last site-wide post (choose-ux-research-agency) and sits next to the SaaS industry page keyword "SaaS UI UX design agency". Rule: do not default every new silo's BOFU post to "how to choose an agency". Look for a buyer-action query the hub does not own (for SaaS: "saas product redesign", which is the engagement itself) and park the agency-choice topic in competitors.json as a deferred candidate.
- 2026-10-06. Evidence: industry-flavoured hubs (saas-ux-design) share entities with their industry page (content/industries/saas.json), and that page lists its own `keywords[]` array ("SaaS onboarding UX", "B2B SaaS design", "admin panel design") that the hub's `sections` do not show. Rule: for any hub with `relatedIndustries`, read the industry page's `keywords[]` and H2s along with the hub, and grep every services JSON for the candidate's main noun (e.g. "redesign") to catch FAQ-level overlaps such as web-app-design-services' "Can you redesign an existing web app in phases?". Record each overlap and the wording to avoid in the brief `notes`.
- 2026-10-06. Evidence: hub section anchors come from `sectionId()` in components/sections/SemanticSections.tsx (lowercase h2, non-alphanumerics to "-"), which gave `/services/saas-ux-design/#how-much-does-saas-ux-design-cost`. Rule: when a post must mention a price or a fact the hub owns, put a deep link to the hub section in `internalTargets` (derive the id with that rule) so the writer links instead of restating the content.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign, QA): the brief set "saas product redesign" as BOFU, but all 6 SERP pages it listed were how-to and best-practice guides, and the post that came out was a planning guide. funnel.json calls that MOFU ("planning the work"); its BOFU examples are cost, choosing an agency and pricing. Rule: set the funnel stage from the SERP's dominant page type, not from how close the topic is to the engagement. If the top results are how-to guides, it is MOFU. Pick BOFU only when cost, pricing, agency or vendor-comparison pages dominate.
- 2026-10-06 (onpage audit). Evidence: the on-page script takes each industry page's keyword from its `anchor` ("Finance UI UX design", "Startups UI UX design"), but both metaTitles used another word ("Fintech…", "Startup…"), so both pages scored 83 until they were retitled. A cannibalisation check that greps metaTitles alone misses this. Rule: when you read an industry or service page for overlap, compare its `anchor` with its `metaTitle` and record the owned phrase from the `anchor`. Give each brief the exact keyword string the title and description must contain (question words included). Good: `"keyword": "how to choose a ux research agency"` plus the note "description opens with this exact phrase". Bad: only the noun phrase "UX research agency".
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-services, first BOFU). Evidence: the ux-audit hub answers cost, what is included, process, report contents, duration and "UX audit vs usability testing", which blocks every funnel.json BOFU example ("ux audit pricing") and the usual deliverables/report queries. The provider-type query "ux audit agency vs freelancer" (SERP: flowpoint, codewave, 925studios, orbix provider-type section, Fiverr gigs) was still open and counted as BOFU vendor comparison. Rule: when a hub has already absorbed the cost/deliverable questions, test "<service> agency vs freelancer / in-house / AI tool" as the BOFU candidate before falling back to "how to choose an agency"; it keeps buyer intent and gives a different post type (Comparison).
- 2026-10-07. Evidence: the generic "Freelancer, in-house hire, or design agency" comparison and the published price/team-size lines live in content/home.ts, not in a content JSON, and home.ts says the UX audit uses "1-2 designers" while the hub says 2 designers review independently. A grep of content/*.json alone misses both. Rule: include content/home.ts in every cannibalisation and fact grep; when the brief leans on a company process or price claim, check that home.ts and the hub agree and flag any conflict in `notes` for the orchestrator.
- 2026-10-07 (onpage audit, ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer). Evidence: the brief told the writer to put the exact phrase "ux audit agency vs freelancer" in the "first sentence" without saying how a "vs" string fits a sentence. The draft opened "Before you settle UX audit agency vs freelancer, …", which the auditor had to fix after publishing. Rule: for any comparison ("X vs Y") or bare-noun-string keyword, give the brief a `keywordCarrier` note with one natural sentence pattern for the intro and one for the description. Good: "carrier: 'the ux audit agency vs freelancer decision'; description opens 'UX audit agency vs freelancer: …'". Bad: "put the exact keyword in the first sentence" with no pattern.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-services, first BOFU). Evidence: the hub owned cost, timeline, deliverables, benefits, "vs UI kit" and "should a startup build one"; "design system agency" is the hub's head term in agency form, the last site-wide post was a "vs" Comparison, and "design system audit" had an all-how-to SERP (MOFU). No page on the site used "ROI" or "payback" as a keyword. "design system roi" kept cost-family buyer intent (page 1 had vendor ROI calculators and agency ROI pages) and turned the hub's price into an input instead of repeating it. Rule: when a hub already publishes its price, test "<service> ROI / payback / business case" as the BOFU candidate. The hub price becomes the cost input (deep link), so the post does not cannibalise the hub. Because ROI SERPs mix calculators with formula guides, write the funnel reasoning and the MOFU-fallback risk into `funnelWhy`.
- 2026-10-08. Evidence: a grep of the central entity across industries and sibling services found 2 conflicts that home.ts did not show. The design-system hub says a full system fits at "2 or more teams", while industries/startups.json says "3 or more squads… at Series B". ui-ux-design-services-for-startups sells a "starter design system" (tokens + 20–30 components), which this hub calls a UI kit. Rule: grep the hub's central entity across ALL of content/services, content/industries and home.ts for numeric thresholds, price labels and naming. Tell the writer which page's wording to follow and list every conflict in `notes`.
- 2026-10-08. Evidence: a grep of content/case-studies/ for "design system|component library|tokens" returned nothing, so the silo has no DOWN link and no real result to quote. Rule: grep the case studies for the hub's entity before outlining. If there is no match, state "no DOWN link, no client results" in `notes`, label any worked example as illustrative with invented inputs, and do not plan a mini-case intro.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi, QA loop 1). Evidence: `keywordCarrier.pattern` asked for "about 20–35 uses in 2,000 words", which is 3–5% density for a 3-word keyword under the word-share measure QA uses (uses × phrase words ÷ body words). The writer followed it and QA failed the post at 2.3%. Rule: state density targets as word share and as a use count derived from it: max uses = 0.02 × expected words ÷ words in the keyword (about 13–15 for a 3-word keyword in 2,000–2,300 words).
- 2026-10-08 (onpage audit, design-system-roi). Evidence: `keywordCarrier.description` in the brief gave the full description text ("… a one-page business case your CFO can approve."). It had no next step, and the writer and QA copied it unchanged, so the post shipped without one. Rule: when a brief supplies a description, write all of it: keyword first, then what the reader gets, then a next step that matches `funnel` (BOFU: "Then request a fixed-scope proposal."; MOFU: case study or consultation; TOFU: the checklist), in 140–158 chars with no apostrophe near the limit. Otherwise supply only the opening pattern. Good: "Design system ROI, step by step: … one-page CFO business case. Then request a fixed-scope proposal." (156). Bad: a description that ends on the contents list.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-services, first BOFU). Evidence: the hub owned cost, deliverables, process, duration and phased redesign. "web app design agency" is the head term in agency form. "web app redesign cost" fell under the hub's cost H2, and "legacy web app redesign" had an all-how-to SERP (netguru, thinkcompany, theskinsfactory), which makes it MOFU. "web app design rfp" was open: every ranking RFP page was written for marketing websites (wapiti, emulent, vitaldesign) or for app development (simpalm, chopdawg), and none scoped design work. Rule: when cost, agency choice and vs-angles are blocked or were used recently, test "<service> RFP / scope of work" as the BOFU procurement candidate. Check whether the ranking RFP pages are website-focused or dev-focused. Build the design version around the hub's price drivers (deep-link the cost section) so the post does not restate them, and choose the type Checklist/template for variety.
- 2026-10-09. Evidence: two scope conflicts for the web app hub sat outside its own JSON. The digital-product-design-services table "Digital product design vs web app design" limits web app design to an "Existing web application", but the web app hub lists "A new web application is planned" as a reason to hire. The home.ts FAQ "We deliver design and development" links to the web app hub, while the digital-product FAQ says the designers do not write code. Rule: grep every services JSON for "vs <this hub's title>" comparison rows and every home.ts FAQ that links to the hub. Sibling pages define this hub's scope, so list any contradiction in `notes` and tell the writer to stay silent on the disputed claim (e.g. whether the agency builds).
- 2026-10-09. Evidence: content/authors.json has empty bios, so author roles exist only in the content/home.ts team list (e.g. Sahar Asif "Senior Manager UX | KAM", Shahmir Farooq "Sr. Communication Designer"). A procurement/RFP topic fits the key-account role better than the authors who had not written yet. Rule: read the roles from home.ts before picking an author. Choose by role first and rotation second (an author last used 3 or more posts ago is fine). Name the rotation candidates you rejected and the topic they would suit, in `authorWhy`.
- 2026-10-09 (onpage audit, location pages). Evidence: city keywords in "<service> <city>" order ("ux design agency philadelphia", "ux design agency chicago") were stacked in front of another noun on 2 pages ("a UX design agency Philadelphia project", "a UX design agency Chicago engagement"). The 10-07 `keywordCarrier` rule covered only "vs" and bare-noun blog keywords. Rule: give every reversed-order keyword (place name or qualifier last) a `keywordCarrier` too, whether it goes in a blog brief or a location spec. Good: "carrier: 'a UX design agency <City> teams can reach'; compounds use '<City> UX design project'". Bad: no carrier, so the writer builds "<keyword> project/cost/FAQs".
- 2026-10-10 (digital-product-design-services, first BOFU). Evidence: "product discovery phase cost" had a clean BOFU SERP (perfectiongeeks pricing page, phenomenon and netguru service pages), but the site publishes no discovery-only price, so a cost post would have needed invented numbers. One word changed the funnel. "product discovery phase" and "is a product discovery phase worth it" returned explainers (dzone, amplitude, productschool, logrocket), while "is a discovery phase worth it" returned vendor pages arguing cost and value (lowcode.agency, acquaintsoft, dogtownmedia). Rule: before choosing a "<phase> cost" BOFU, check that the site publishes a price for that exact phase. If it does not, test the buy/skip version ("is a <phase> worth it") and search 2–3 wordings of it. Then pick the wording whose SERP is vendor-dominated, and record the variants that flip to MOFU in `funnelWhy`.
- 2026-10-10. Evidence: 7 of the 8 new case studies list a scope line, and "discovery" appears in the scope of Parents Connect and TradeZella. Both are product revamps, so they do not fit a new-product post. Nitro, Vocable and Digno describe what discovery actually did in their body text (stakeholder interviews -> user flows -> whitepaper; the creator journey mapped from interviews; scoring requirements from stakeholders and users). Elia Wellness says "idea to product" but has no discovery detail. Rule: choose case studies for a post by what the body text describes, not by keywords in the scope line. For each case the brief cites, list the exact sentences and numbers the writer may use, and write "do not attribute the result to <phase> alone".
- 2026-10-10. Evidence: competitors.json still told a future web-app brief to use a case study as a "DOWN link", though the owner had removed that case study on 2026-10-09 (RULES.md honesty list). Rule: when you update competitors.json, scan its `notes` for clients on the RULES.md removed list and replace each mention with a pointer to a real case study in content/case-studies/.
- 2026-10-10 (is-discovery-phase-worth-it, QA). Evidence: `keywordCarrier.description` said "Use as written" for "Use our 6-question skip test, see what it hands to design, then request…". Here "it" grammatically refers to the skip test, which hands nothing to design. QA had to FIX it. Rule: in a prescribed description, read every pronoun back to the nearest noun before it. If that noun is wrong, name the subject ("see what discovery hands design").
- 2026-10-11 (mobile-app-ui-ux-design-services, first BOFU). Evidence: the orchestrator suggested a testimonial-quote intro. Only 2 of the case studies cited for the silo carry a quote, and neither praises our design work. The Anyday quote comes from Crystal Yeoman of Boston Pizza, a customer of the client, and is about the payout product. The Elia Wellness quote thanks the team for "building" the platform, which feeds the open home.ts "design and development" conflict. Rule: before you plan a quote intro or a testimonial line, read `quoteAuthor` and the quote itself. Use a quote only if the speaker is the client and the quote praises work the hub sells. Otherwise write "do not present as a testimonial about us" in `notes` and choose another intro.
- 2026-10-11. Evidence: "<service> agency vs freelancer" was the strongest open BOFU query for the mobile silo, 4 posts after ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer used the same frame. The last-3 variety check passes on all 7 dimensions, so it alone would not catch the repeat. Rule: also name the nearest structural relative anywhere on the site in `variety.differsFrom` and list what the new post must NOT reuse from it (intro style, matrix, hybrid flow, scope sheet). Build the outline around work that only this hub's service contains (here: two platform guidelines, test rounds, test-build review). Never rebuild the generic provider matrix.
- 2026-10-12 (usability-testing-services, first BOFU). Evidence: the hub owns the total cost H2 ($25,000–$60,000), and the orchestrator ruled out agency-vs-X, how-to-choose, RFP and 5-users formats. "how many rounds of usability testing" looked open, but its page 1 was mostly "how many users" pages (userbrain, trymata, koji, uxtweak), so Google treats it as the hub-owned and banned participant-count question. "usability testing cost per participant" had a cost-dominated SERP (measuringu, two cleverx cost guides, NN/g recruiting cost) and a unit (the participant) that the hub never prices. Rule: (a) when a hub owns the total price, test the per-unit cost query ("<service> cost per participant / per round / per screen"). Build it from sourced market inputs, keep the hub range as the only price we state, and deep-link the hub cost section. (b) Before choosing any candidate, read the page-1 titles. If half or more answer a question the hub or a ban already owns, reject the candidate whatever its wording.
- 2026-10-12. Evidence: the last 7 fingerprints had already used all six intro styles named in RULES.md (scenario, data point, myth, question, definition, mini case), plus "contrarian take". Rule: when the RULES.md rotation is exhausted, choose an intro that comes from the post's own artefact (here an "itemized receipt" for one participant). List the last-7 styles in `variety.introStyle` so QA can see the new style is not a repeat.
- 2026-10-12. Evidence: the usability hub lists "participant recruitment" as a deliverable but never says whether participant incentives sit inside the $25,000–$60,000 price. Only one case study (TradeZella: "tested with traders before launch") mentions testing in its body; Elia Wellness (adults 50+) does not. home.ts has an unnamed "telehealth … elderly patients, tested on tablet at the bedside" claim with no case study behind it. Rule: when a post's topic is one line of the price (incentives, licences, travel, fixes), check whether the hub says that line is included. If the hub is silent, write a CONFLICT in `notes` and forbid the writer from stating either way. For audience-specific proof, grep the case-study bodies for the method verb ("tested", "interviewed"), and mark home.ts anecdotes without a case study as do-not-use.
- 2026-10-12 (onpage audit, usability-testing-cost-per-participant): the brief marked backlink suggestion 3 (UX research hub cost paragraph) as "Optional… Lower priority", while also confirming it was an allowed source with a real topic match. The pipeline read "optional" as "skip", so the link was missing at publish, the second time in 2 days (10-11: the home FAQ link). Rule: give every `backlinkSuggestions` entry a `priority` of `required` or `skip` (with a reason), and use `conditional` only with a named blocker. Never write "optional". Bad: `"change": "Optional. Link …", "why": "Lower priority: …"`. Good: `"priority": "required", "why": "Allowed source, real topic match (clinicians on the ladder); sends one link out of the silo, accepted."`. If two entries are alternatives on the same page (suggestions 1 and 2 here), mark one `required` and the other `skip: alternative to #1`.
