## Lessons (learned from runs, QA, audits and results)

<!-- lessons below -->

- 2026-10-05 (ux-research-services, first BOFU). Evidence: the hub JSON has its own H2 "How much does UX research cost?" with a price range, so "ux research cost" would cannibalise it even though the hub's metaTitle does not say "cost". Rule: check the hub's `sections[].h2` and FAQ questions, not only `keyword`/`metaTitle`. If a hub H2 already answers the query, reject it as a post keyword and use a buyer-evaluation angle (how to choose, compare proposals) that links up to the hub's section.
- 2026-10-05. Evidence: Semrush returned `no_api_units` on the first call and WebFetch was blocked by the egress proxy for every agency blog tried (fuselabcreative, ux4sight, answerlab, 925studios, eleken). Rule: test Semrush with one call before planning around it; when WebFetch is blocked, use `<domain> <post title>` WebSearch queries to get each page's coverage from snippets, set `volume`/`kd` to null, and state the data source and its limits in the brief's `dataSource` field. Never estimate volume or KD.
- 2026-10-05. Evidence: SERP snippets for agency-hiring queries carried unsourced numbers ("3x more likely to report satisfaction"). Rule: list such claims in the brief `notes` as "do not use" so the writer does not pick them up from the competitor page.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-ux-design, first BOFU). Evidence: the obvious BOFU query "how to choose a SaaS UX design agency" had a weak, beatable SERP, but it would repeat the format of the last site-wide post (choose-ux-research-agency) and sits next to the SaaS industry page keyword "SaaS UI UX design agency". Rule: do not default every new silo's BOFU post to "how to choose an agency". Look for a buyer-action query the hub does not own (for SaaS: "saas product redesign", which is the engagement itself) and park the agency-choice topic in competitors.json as a deferred candidate.
- 2026-10-06. Evidence: industry-flavoured hubs (saas-ux-design) share entities with their industry page (content/industries/saas.json), and that page lists its own `keywords[]` array ("SaaS onboarding UX", "B2B SaaS design", "admin panel design") that the hub's `sections` do not show. Rule: for any hub with `relatedIndustries`, read the industry page's `keywords[]` and H2s along with the hub, and grep every services JSON for the candidate's main noun (e.g. "redesign") to catch FAQ-level overlaps such as web-app-design-services' "Can you redesign an existing web app in phases?". Record each overlap and the wording to avoid in the brief `notes`.
- 2026-10-06. Evidence: hub section anchors come from `sectionId()` in components/sections/SemanticSections.tsx (lowercase h2, non-alphanumerics to "-"), which gave `/services/saas-ux-design/#how-much-does-saas-ux-design-cost`. Rule: when a post must mention a price or a fact the hub owns, put a deep link to the hub section in `internalTargets` (derive the id with that rule) so the writer links instead of restating the content.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign, QA): the brief set "saas product redesign" as BOFU, but all 6 SERP pages it listed were how-to and best-practice guides, and the post that came out was a planning guide. funnel.json calls that MOFU ("planning the work"); its BOFU examples are cost, choosing an agency and pricing. Rule: set the funnel stage from the SERP's dominant page type, not from how close the topic is to the engagement. If the top results are how-to guides, it is MOFU. Pick BOFU only when cost, pricing, agency or vendor-comparison pages dominate.
- 2026-10-06 (onpage audit). Evidence: the on-page script takes each industry page's keyword from its `anchor` ("Finance UI UX design", "Startups UI UX design"), but both metaTitles used another word ("Fintech…", "Startup…"), so both pages scored 83 until they were retitled. A cannibalisation check that greps metaTitles alone misses this. Rule: when you read an industry or service page for overlap, compare its `anchor` with its `metaTitle` and record the owned phrase from the `anchor`. Give each brief the exact keyword string the title and description must contain (question words included). Good: `"keyword": "how to choose a ux research agency"` plus the note "description opens with this exact phrase". Bad: only the noun phrase "UX research agency".
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-services, first BOFU). Evidence: the ux-audit hub answers cost, what is included, process, report contents, duration and "UX audit vs usability testing", which blocks every funnel.json BOFU example ("ux audit pricing") and the usual deliverables/report queries. The provider-type query "ux audit agency vs freelancer" (SERP: flowpoint, codewave, 925studios, orbix provider-type section, Fiverr gigs) was still open and counted as BOFU vendor comparison. Rule: when a hub has already absorbed the cost/deliverable questions, test "<service> agency vs freelancer / in-house / AI tool" as the BOFU candidate before falling back to "how to choose an agency"; it keeps buyer intent and gives a different post type (Comparison).
- 2026-10-07. Evidence: the generic "Freelancer, in-house hire, or design agency" comparison and the published price/team-size lines live in content/home.ts, not in a content JSON, and home.ts says the UX audit uses "1-2 designers" while the hub says 2 designers review independently. A grep of content/*.json alone misses both. Rule: include content/home.ts in every cannibalisation and fact grep; when the brief leans on a company process or price claim, check that home.ts and the hub agree and flag any conflict in `notes` for the orchestrator.
- 2026-10-07 (onpage audit, ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer). Evidence: the brief told the writer to put the exact phrase "ux audit agency vs freelancer" in the "first sentence" without saying how a "vs" string fits a sentence. The draft opened "Before you settle UX audit agency vs freelancer, …", which the auditor had to fix after publishing. Rule: for any comparison ("X vs Y") or bare-noun-string keyword, give the brief a `keywordCarrier` note with one natural sentence pattern for the intro and one for the description. Good: "carrier: 'the ux audit agency vs freelancer decision'; description opens 'UX audit agency vs freelancer: …'". Bad: "put the exact keyword in the first sentence" with no pattern.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-services, first BOFU). Evidence: the hub owned cost, timeline, deliverables, benefits, "vs UI kit" and "should a startup build one"; "design system agency" is the hub's head term in agency form, the last site-wide post was a "vs" Comparison, and "design system audit" had an all-how-to SERP (MOFU). No page on the site used "ROI" or "payback" as a keyword. "design system roi" kept cost-family buyer intent (page 1 had vendor ROI calculators and agency ROI pages) and turned the hub's price into an input instead of repeating it. Rule: when a hub already publishes its price, test "<service> ROI / payback / business case" as the BOFU candidate. The hub price becomes the cost input (deep link), so the post does not cannibalise the hub. Because ROI SERPs mix calculators with formula guides, write the funnel reasoning and the MOFU-fallback risk into `funnelWhy`.
- 2026-10-08. Evidence: a grep of the central entity across industries and sibling services found 2 conflicts that home.ts did not show. The design-system hub says a full system fits at "2 or more teams", while industries/startups.json says "3 or more squads… at Series B". ui-ux-design-services-for-startups sells a "starter design system" (tokens + 20–30 components), which this hub calls a UI kit. Rule: grep the hub's central entity across ALL of content/services, content/industries and home.ts for numeric thresholds, price labels and naming. Tell the writer which page's wording to follow and list every conflict in `notes`.
- 2026-10-08. Evidence: a grep of content/case-studies/ for "design system|component library|tokens" returned nothing, so the silo has no DOWN link and no real result to quote. Rule: grep the case studies for the hub's entity before outlining. If there is no match, state "no DOWN link, no client results" in `notes`, label any worked example as illustrative with invented inputs, and do not plan a mini-case intro.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi, QA loop 1). Evidence: `keywordCarrier.pattern` asked for "about 20–35 uses in 2,000 words", which is 3–5% density for a 3-word keyword under the word-share measure QA uses (uses × phrase words ÷ body words). The writer followed it and QA failed the post at 2.3%. Rule: state density targets as word share and as a use count derived from it: max uses = 0.02 × expected words ÷ words in the keyword (about 13–15 for a 3-word keyword in 2,000–2,300 words).
- 2026-10-08 (onpage audit, design-system-roi). Evidence: `keywordCarrier.description` in the brief gave the full description text ("… a one-page business case your CFO can approve."). It had no next step, and the writer and QA copied it unchanged, so the post shipped without one. Rule: when a brief supplies a description, write all of it: keyword first, then what the reader gets, then a next step that matches `funnel` (BOFU: "Then request a fixed-scope proposal."; MOFU: case study or consultation; TOFU: the checklist), in 140–158 chars with no apostrophe near the limit. Otherwise supply only the opening pattern. Good: "Design system ROI, step by step: … one-page CFO business case. Then request a fixed-scope proposal." (156). Bad: a description that ends on the contents list.
