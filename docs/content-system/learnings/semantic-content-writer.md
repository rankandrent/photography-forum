# Learnings: semantic-content-writer

Lessons this agent has learned. Read before every run; add new lessons at the end of every run.
Each lesson: date, what happened (evidence), the rule to follow next time. Keep the file under
80 lessons; merge duplicates and delete lessons proven wrong.

## Owner rules (highest priority — written by the site owner, never removed by agents)

<!-- add owner rules here, e.g. "- Always prefer US cost/pricing keywords for the SaaS silo." -->
- 2026-10-06 (owner): A post must be semantically complete: every heading fully answered (answer → detail → example), every brief point covered with substance, no unfinished paragraphs or thin FAQs. Follow QA's "Guidance for the writer" item by item in repair rounds.
- 2026-10-06 (owner, REPLACES the 2026-10-05 duplicate rule): Owner prefers IMAGES over text. For every table, checklist, step list, framework or comparison that would make a good infographic, put a `<!-- visual: … -->` marker right before it and wrap the table/list itself in `<details class="astext"><summary>Show as text</summary>` … `</details>` (blank lines inside). Never leave the same content visible twice. Aim for 4–6 visuals.
- 2026-10-05 (owner): Never place a `<!-- visual: … -->` marker next to a table or list that already shows the same content. In choose-ux-research-agency the scorecard, brief checklist, red-flag and proposal images all repeated the post's own tables/lists and had to be removed after publishing. Ask for a visual only when it shows something the text cannot: an overview flow of the whole process, a decision tree, relationships, a timeline, a before/after or a 2×2.
- 2026-10-05 (owner): Keep the bold extractive answer under each H2 to one sentence of at most ~30 words. Long bold blocks hurt readability; put the detail in the normal paragraph that follows.
- 2026-10-08 (escalated by onpage-seo-auditor, repeat gap, not an owner rule; owner may edit): the `description` ends with the reader's next step, in 140–158 chars, using the site's offer words ("request a fixed-scope proposal", "book a free consultation", or the TOFU checklist). Evidence: 3 of 4 published posts (choose-ux-research-agency, saas-product-redesign, design-system-roi) and the home page shipped without one, despite the 2026-10-06 lesson. If the brief gives a description without a next step, rewrite it. Never call the proposal "free". Good: "Design system ROI, step by step: baseline hours, build and upkeep cost, payback month and a one-page CFO business case. Then request a fixed-scope proposal." Bad: "… payback month and a one-page business case your CFO can approve." (ends on the contents list). Update 2026-10-09: when the full offer clause does not fit, end with the verb of the post's own closing CTA in 3–4 words, and shorten the contents list to nouns instead of dropping items. Good: "… recruitment tests and data terms. Send us your brief." (154, the closing anchor is "Send us your research brief"). Bad: dropping "and admins" or "roll out behind gates" to fit "Then book a free consultation."
- 2026-10-08 (escalated by onpage-seo-auditor, repeat gap, not an owner rule): every sentence that holds the exact keyword must read as correct English when spoken aloud. Evidence: on 10-07 the keyword had no carrier ("settle UX audit agency vs freelancer"), and on 10-08 a dangling participle ("Below is how to calculate design system ROI step by step, priced against…"). Both scored 100 on `onpage`. Before handing off, read out the title, H1, description, first sentence, every bold answer and every H2.
- 2026-10-09 (escalated by onpage-seo-auditor, repeat gap, not an owner rule; applies to blog AND location/service pages): a keyword must never be used as a modifier stacked onto another noun. Evidence: 10-07 "settle UX audit agency vs freelancer" (blog intro), and 10-09 /location/ux-design-agency-philadelphia/ H2 "How much does a UX design agency Philadelphia project cost?" and its bold answer "A UX design agency Philadelphia project costs…". Both scored 100. Rule: when a keyword ends in a place name or a "vs" pair, use it only as a whole noun phrase with an article ("a UX design agency Philadelphia teams can reach"), and rebuild compound uses in natural order ("a Philadelphia UX design project"). Test: swap the keyword for "this agency"; "a this agency project" shows the defect. Update 2026-10-10 (3rd and 4th repeat): /location/ux-design-agency-columbus/ shipped "Our UX design agency Columbus work spans…" and "A UX design agency Columbus engagement with us costs…". is-discovery-phase-worth-it shipped the secondary n-gram as a bare subject: "so discovery phase vs MVP is a false choice". The rule covers secondary "X vs Y" n-grams too: give them a carrier verb or noun. Good: "so framing it as discovery phase vs MVP sets up a false choice." Bad: "so discovery phase vs MVP is a false choice." Before handoff, run `grep -niE '[^ ]+ vs [^ ]+' content/blog/<slug>.md` and read each hit with "this choice" swapped in. Update 2026-10-11 (5th and 6th repeat): mobile-app-design-agency-vs-freelancer shipped the closing "Weighing a mobile app design agency vs freelancer usually ends with…". The "this choice" swap passes ("Weighing this choice"), but read aloud it is "a [agency] versus [bare] freelancer". /location/ux-design-agency-austin/ shipped "Our UX design agency Austin practice covers…". Parity rule: when "a/an/the" sits before the first half of a "vs" pair used as two objects, the second half needs its own article, or switch to "against a". If you cannot add the article without breaking the exact string, use the string only with a head noun ("the X vs Y question/decision"). Good: "Weighing a mobile app design agency against a freelancer usually ends…" or "The mobile app design agency vs freelancer question usually ends…". Bad: "Weighing a mobile app design agency vs freelancer usually ends…".
- 2026-10-12 (owner): the primary keyword must be in the metaTitle, the description, the H1, the first 60 words and at least 40% of the H2s, and be the most-mentioned entity on the page. Cover at least 80% of the brief's `entities` and 70% of its `attributes` in visible text (not only in images or collapsed text), each with a real fact or explanation, never a bare list of names. Content must be useful first: no padding to hit counts.
- 2026-10-12 (owner, layout-aware pages): follow RULES.md "Layout-aware pages". Each H2 is followed by its bold text answer, never an image. Put one sentence before every table saying what it compares. Bold only the direct answer and key terms. Every key fact appears once in open prose; image text versions are visible panels now, so do not repeat the same list a third time.
- 2026-10-12 (owner): service, industry and location section detail text is 50–70 words (90 max). Keep every named entity and attribute and the "no local office" line; cut filler. The owner found 100+ word sections too heavy once "Read more" was removed.
- 2026-10-14 (owner, concept bindings, RULES.md "Concept bindings"): on every new blog, industry or location page, use each brief `bindings.synonyms` entry at least once, name the parent in the intro or first section, and give each child its own H3, list item or table row. Keep composite concepts whole and define each in one sentence when first used. The first sentence under every H2 names the central entity (or a synonym) plus one `essential` concept. Put the context word next to every `ambiguous` word. Cover every `queryVariants` entry in an H2 or FAQ with the same answer, and use only the `siteFacts` numbers. Good H2 opener: "An e-commerce UX audit checks the checkout, search and product pages against usability heuristics." Bad: "It usually starts with data."

## Lessons (learned from runs, QA, audits and results)

<!-- lessons below -->

- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency): a brief with 10 outline H2s, 3 tables and 5 FAQs produced a ~2,450-word first draft against the 2,200 cap, and three trim passes were needed. Rule: when the outline has 9+ H2s, budget about 150 words per H2 (tables included) and 40 words per FAQ before drafting, and write each section to that budget.
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency): no Bash tool was available, so word counts came from a Grep `-o` token listing paged with `offset`, which also counts frontmatter (~170 words) and HTML comment markers (~150 words here). Rule: subtract both before reporting body word count, and say in the report how the count was made.
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency): a 1–2% density target for a 4-word-plus keyword ("how to choose a ux research agency") would be stuffing; the first draft had only 6 body mentions of the central entity. Rule: apply density to the central entity phrase (here "UX research agency", aiming for 15+ mentions in ~2,000 words), use the exact long keyword 2–3 times, and put the entity in H2 answer sentences, where it reads naturally.
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency, QA): a closing paragraph placed after the last `### FAQ` answer was rendered into that question's FAQPage JSON-LD answer, and it repeated the CTA just above. Rule: nothing goes after the last FAQ answer. Put any closing line before `## FAQs`, or drop it when the last section already ends with the CTA.
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency, QA): the generative/evaluative bullets reused the hub's definition sentence word for word ("generative research finds what to build", "evaluative research checks whether a design works"). The draft also used the British construction "agrees the objectives/research questions". Rule: when the brief says the hub owns a definition, rephrase it from the buyer's angle and never reuse the hub's sentence. In US English write "agree on X".
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency, fact-check): the CTAs promised a "30-minute call with the lead researcher" and "the researcher who would run your study on the line", but the site promises only "a free consultation and a fixed-scope proposal" and lists no researcher role. Rule: before writing any CTA or capability line, grep `content/pages/about.md`, `content/home.ts` and the hub JSON for the exact offer wording, and use only that wording. Never add call lengths, roles or deliverables the site does not state.
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency, fact-check): the rubric line "if the answer is 'our design team,' research is a phase" failed our own agency, because the home page says the designers who run your research stay through engineering. Rule: check every red flag and "strong answer" in a buyer rubric against the site's positioning; a criterion must never disqualify us. Judge the evidence (named moderators, a second review of findings), not the job title.
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency, fact-check): three sourced claims were quietly overstated: the NN/g per-group count was invented ("4–5 per group" vs. the article's 3–4 for two groups and 3 for three or more), the HIPAA BAA was written as always required (it applies only when the client is a covered entity or business associate), and the price was "priced on the same 5 units" when the hub lists 4 price factors. Rule: copy numbers and conditions from the source exactly, keep the source's "only when" qualifiers, and when the post's own framework (e.g. 5 comparison units) differs from the hub's list, name which items the hub actually uses.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign): the first draft placed `<!-- link: … -->` markers in place of the anchor text ("our <!-- link --> engagements", "see our <!-- link -->."), which would leave the rendered sentence without its anchor words. Rule: write the anchor words in the prose and put the marker right after them; read every link marker's sentence with the comment deleted before finishing.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign): with no Bash, an exact token total came from Grep `\S+ -o` with `head_limit: 1` and stepped `offset` values (binary search, 4–6 calls) instead of listing ~2,800 tokens. The first draft still hit ~2,365 body words for 9 H2s + 5 FAQs despite the 150-words-per-H2 lesson, because scope and rollout sections ran 250–300 words. Rule: count with the offset binary search after the first draft, and cap the two heaviest sections (tables or numbered steps plus prose) at ~200 words before writing the lighter ones.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign): the brief asked for both a numbered rollout step list and a "rollout ramp" visual of the same steps, which conflicts with the owner rule on visuals repeating lists. Rule: when a brief's visual duplicates a list in its own section, keep the visual but redefine its idea to what the list cannot show (shape over time, rollback loops), place it after intervening prose, and flag the conflict in the report.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign, QA): "Google HEART framework" went unexpanded although HEART is an acronym. The closing CTA also reused the previous post's formula ("free consultation … fixed-scope proposal"), and the NN/g lead-in "Nielsen Norman Group's guidance is that" was copied from choose-ux-research-agency (both found as shared 5-grams). Rule: expand named-framework acronyms (HEART, JTBD, SUS) on first use. Before finishing, grep the last 3 posts for your CTA sentence and every source lead-in, and reword any shared 5-gram. Use the brief's leadAngle wording for the offer rather than the previous post's.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign, fact-check): the brief's leadAngle itself promised things the site does not offer (a "baseline-and-rollout plan", a "30-minute call with a senior product designer"), and it merged the free consultation with the paid discovery step. Copying it failed the fact-check twice (L78, L160). Rule: treat leadAngle as the angle only. Check every promised deliverable against about.md and the hub, keep "free consultation → fixed-scope proposal / fixed price" separate from "paid discovery step → baseline, target metric per stage", and write process steps the site does not state as advice to the reader ("In week 1, gather…"), not as "we do…".
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign, QA + fact-check): the fact-checker and QA gave different alt texts for the redrawn decision tree ("churn interviews do not point to the UX" vs "users cannot show the cause is UX"), and the SVG was still the old tree when the repair ran. Rule: when two reviewers disagree, use the version that matches the redraw spec the designer will follow (QA owns image specs), update the alt in both the post and visuals.json, and flag that the SVG must be redrawn to match.
- 2026-10-06 (onpage audit, choose-ux-research-agency): the metaTitle had the keyword, but the description opened "Choose a UX research agency with…" and so missed "how" from the keyword "how to choose a ux research agency" (onpage `desc-keyword` failed, 96/100). Rule: open the `description` with the exact keyword phrase, question words included, then say what the reader gets and the next step, in 140–158 characters. Good: "How to choose a UX research agency: a 1-page brief, a 7-criteria scorecard…". Bad: "Choose a UX research agency with a brief…".
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer): the first draft came out at about 2,600 body words for 9 H2s, 5 "Show as text" blocks and 5 FAQs, and it took three trim passes to get to about 2,190. The `<details>` text blocks (a 7-row matrix, a 4-up sample, steps, a scope sheet) added about 350 words that the 150-words-per-H2 budget never counted. Rule: when a post has 4 or more text-behind-image blocks, budget them first (about 60–80 words each) and give the prose under each H2 about 120 words. The count method was the Grep `\S+` offset search, minus frontmatter (195), minus comment markers (counted per marker, about 440 here) and minus markdown tokens (about 125).
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer): the brief told the writer to state "2 independent reviewers" (outline, leadAngle, FAQ hint, internalTargets anchor "our 2-reviewer audit process", coverage-curve visual), but home.ts says "1–2 designers", so the orchestrator banned the number. Rule: when site sources disagree on a capability figure, remove that figure everywhere the brief puts it: prose, CTA, FAQ, link anchors and visual specs (the visual marker says "do not mark a count"). Use the hub's qualitative wording ("independent reviews by more than one evaluator") and list each changed spot in the report.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer): an edit to trim words produced "We genuinely point clients to a freelancer", which implies audit clients when no case study covers an audit. Rule: in a silo with no case study, write routing advice to the reader ("we would point you to…") and never use "clients" or "customers" for that service.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer, QA loop 1): QA failed the post on completeness even though every heading had a bold answer and an example. The failures were at the item level: freelancer-fit and in-house bullets with no "why", 3 of 4 "Ask…" vetting items that never said what a good answer looks like, and matrix cells like "Varies; ask", "Read apart" and "Scheduled around a team". Rule: every bullet carries its reason (a "because"/"so" clause), every "ask X" item says what a good answer looks like, and no table cell is "varies" or a fragment. Write the concrete range, or the question to ask together with what to expect back.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer, QA loop 1): the 4-up sample finding annotated the in-house note "Missing: heuristic, severity, effort", yet by the section's own four-part test (evidence, heuristic, severity, fix + effort) the note also lacked evidence and a fix. Rule: when a post defines a test and then grades examples against it, grade each example part by part against that exact list before you write the annotations.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer, QA loop 1): the closing ("Send us your scope sheet … at no charge and return a fixed-scope proposal … weigh our proposal against every other quote") repeated the CTA of the last two posts ("send your X → free consultation + fixed-scope proposal", "score us against every other agency"). Rule: before you write the closing, read the last 3 posts' final paragraphs and open with an angle that belongs to this post only (here, the reader keeps the scope sheet and uses it with every provider, with a callback to the post's own matrix). Do not open with "Send us your X". Corrected in repair loop 1: the orchestrator rejected QA's "a fixed price, or a note that you do not need us" closing, because neither promise appears on the site. The offer itself must stay in about.md's exact words ("every project begins with a free consultation and a fixed-scope proposal"), so vary the lead, the anchor text and the sentence order, not the offer.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer, fact-check loop 1): the post used secondary paraphrases of three classic findings, and each one drifted from the primary paper. NN/g's "20–50%" is 20–51% in Nielsen and Molich (1990). "3–5 evaluators find 74–87%" applies to usability *specialists* (Nielsen, 1992). Hertzum and Jacobsen's 5–65% is the *average* agreement between pairs. Rule: for a named study's figure, use the primary paper's exact number, population and statistic (average, specialists, per page), name the paper and year in the prose, and never write a past study result as a present-tense law in takeaways or bold answers.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer, fact-check loop 1): one sentence, "our UX audit services sit in the agency column", turned every agency cell of the matrix into a claim about us. "Automated checks plus keyboard and screen-reader passes" and "more than one evaluator" failed because the hub states neither. Rule: when the post places us in a comparison column or a trigger list, check each cell and each bullet against the hub's exact wording. Rewrite unsupported capabilities as the hub's phrase plus a buyer instruction (for example, "WCAG 2.2 AA check of audited screens; ask how it is tested").
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer, QA loop 2): the fact-check repair moved the study detail ("In Jakob Nielsen's heuristic evaluation studies, single evaluators found 20–51%… 3–5 usability specialists found 74–87%") into the bold answer, but left the first prose sentence beneath it, which opened with the same words and figures. The same claim appeared twice back to back. Rule: the sentence after a bold answer adds the how/why (source, method, mechanism). It must never restate the answer's figures. After editing a bold answer, reread the next sentence and cut whatever it repeats.
- 2026-10-07 (onpage audit, ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer): the first sentence forced the exact keyword in as if it were a noun: "Before you settle UX audit agency vs freelancer, weigh one number…". The script scored it 100 because the phrase was present, but a reader trips on it in the most visible sentence of the post. The auditor changed it to "settle the UX audit agency vs freelancer question". Rule: a comparison keyword ("X vs Y") or a bare noun string needs a carrier: a determiner before it and a head noun after it ("the X vs Y question/decision/choice"), or a preposition ("choosing between X and Y" plus the exact phrase once elsewhere in the first 100 words). Test: read the sentence with the keyword replaced by "this choice"; if "Before you settle this choice" sounds wrong, the original is wrong too. Good: "Before you settle the UX audit agency vs freelancer question, …". Bad: "Before you settle UX audit agency vs freelancer, …".
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi): the brief asked for 20–35 uses of "design system ROI", but the first draft had only 6 in prose. Its sections said "the model", "the ROI" or "the calculation" instead, and 5 of the 11 hits were alt text. Rule: after the first draft, grep the exact carrier, ignore alt text and frontmatter, and change generic back-references into the keyword where it fits as a noun or a compound modifier. Good places are bold answers, section openers and "your X model/sheet" phrases ("most design system ROI models go soft", "push design system ROI below zero"). Check that each one still reads correctly when you swap in "this".
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi): the worked example was calculated by hand before drafting (cumulative cost and savings per month, for each scenario). But the first draft still added a lever claim nobody had checked: "Halve the upkeep hours or speed adoption by a quarter and the picture changes". When recomputed, faster adoption did not move the low scenario at all. Halving owner hours moved it to month 39. Rule: every "change X and the result changes" sentence in an illustrative model gets its own recomputation and states the resulting number; drop any lever you have not computed.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi): the brief described the Sparkbox result as "about 47% faster", but the source's raw medians (4.2 h vs 2 h) mean 52% less time, and WebFetch failed with DNS errors on every source, including BLS, Sparkbox, Figma and Smashing. Rule: when a brief gives a derived percentage, recompute it from the source's raw figures; when they disagree, quote the raw figures, not the percentage. When fetches fail, verify through WebSearch snippets and tell the report which figures only secondary sites confirm (here, the BLS software developer median of $133,080).
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi, QA loop 1): following the brief's "20–35 uses", the draft reached 17 exact uses + 1 variant in 2,360 words. That is 2.3% by the site's word-share measure (uses × phrase words ÷ body words) and 2.6% of the open text, over the 1–2% cap. The extra uses were the mechanical "design system ROI model/sheet/estimate" back-references. Rule: compute word-share density yourself before handing off, and stop at 2.0% whatever count the brief gives. For a 3-word keyword in ~2,300 words that is ≤ 15 uses, variants included.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi, QA loop 1): the worked example had no migration row and no adoption-support line, yet the next H2 lists both among the "5 costs most models leave out". The paragraph under the decision tree ("run the model when 2 or more signals hold") let a 1-team product through, while the tree sends it to a UI kit. Rule: after drafting, check the illustrative model against every cost and rule the post itself lists (add the row or say why it is omitted, with the recomputed number), and walk the prose under a tree through each tree path.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi, fact-check loop 1): the assumption row said "$85 blended (3 engineers to 1 designer, BLS medians ÷ 0.70)", but the post's own figures give (3 × 92 + 68) ÷ 4 = $86. The label claimed a derivation the number did not follow. "The one public timing test" was also false, because Figma had published a timed test as well. Rule: when a model input is rounded or picked by hand, label it as "an assumption rounded from X" and show the derivation's inputs. Recompute every "derived from" label. Never write "the one / the only" about published studies; write "one".
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi, fact-check loop 1): three sentences described tools or our engagement beyond their sources. Chromatic "catches drift from the Figma library" (it compares against the last approved snapshot). Figma "detaches per team" (it reports detaches per component). "Buys the build, gets its baseline" (the engagement delivers only the UI inventory). Rule: describe a tool by what its docs say it compares or counts, at the same level of detail (per team vs per component). Describe what an engagement hands over with the hub's deliverable list, not the post's broader concept.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi, repair loop 1): the closing said "Book a free consultation … every project begins with a free consultation", which is the offer twice. That exact clause was also the 3rd closing on the site to use it. Fix: about.md's exact clause appears once; the lead-in comes from the post's own model ("the build fee is the one row your own data cannot fill"); and the /contact/ anchor no longer contains "free consultation" ("bring your baseline sheet to us"). Rule: when the offer clause is fixed, never put "free consultation" in the CTA anchor as well. Vary the anchor and the lead-in instead.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi, QA loop 2): two repair sentences 26 lines apart both said "in this illustrative sensitivity check" (L124, L150). The new closing claimed "the build fee is the one row your own data cannot fill", which the post's own tooling row (vendor quotes) contradicts. Rule: when pasting several repair sentences, give each a different qualifier. Test every "the one / only" claim against the post's own inputs table before handing back.
- 2026-10-08 (onpage audit, design-system-roi): the intro said "Below is how to calculate design system ROI step by step, priced against the published fee…". Here "priced" has no noun to attach to, because "how to calculate" is not a thing that gets priced. Rule: a participle after a comma ("priced", "based", "built") must describe the noun right before the comma. If that noun is "how to X" or a verb phrase, rebuild the clause with "with the <noun> <participle>". Good: "Below is a step-by-step way to calculate design system ROI, with the build priced against the published fee…". Bad: "Below is how to calculate design system ROI step by step, priced against…".
- 2026-10-08 (onpage audit, design-system-roi): a bold answer opened with a digit ("**4 metrics prove design system ROI after launch:**"), and one H2 paired "when" with "never" ("When does a design system never pay back?"), while its answer says "does not pay back". Rule: spell out a number that starts a sentence or a bold answer ("Four metrics…"; digits are fine mid-sentence). Phrase a question H2 with the same verb its answer uses ("When does a design system not pay back?"). Test: `grep -nE '^(\*\*)?[0-9]+ ' content/blog/<slug>.md` returns nothing.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp): the brief asked the writer to cite "the 18F state field guide's point that a custom-software RFP can stay under 20 pages". WebFetch failed (DNS) on 18f.gov, and two exact-phrase searches found only secondary mentions of "a dozen pages" (2019 solicitation guide) and an "18-page" RFQ (2015 press), never "under 20 pages". The figure was dropped, the length stayed labelled as our recommendation, and 18F was cited only for claims a snippet quoted (strengths, weaknesses and risks, then a verbal interview, via FedScoop; performance-based contracts, via the guide's contents). Rule: when a brief gives a figure from a source you cannot open, search the exact phrase; if no snippet carries it, drop the figure and flag it in the report rather than citing a near match.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp): the brief put the same fact in a section point and an FAQ ("6–10 page main document plus attachments"), and the first draft stated it in near-identical words in both places. Rule: when the brief places a fact in a section and an FAQ, give it in full once (the FAQ, which feeds FAQPage schema) and point to it from the section ("the FAQs give our length guideline").
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp): the orchestrator allowed "free consultation" only once (inside about.md's exact clause in the closing), but the brief's mid-post invitation said "bring the grid half-filled to a free consultation" and listed /contact/ there too. Rule: when the offer clause is once-only, reword every brief invitation that names the consultation ("bring the half-filled grid to a first call with us"), keep the /contact/ link to the closing on BOFU posts, and grep `free consultation` for a count of exactly 1 before handing off.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp, QA loop 1): two secondary n-grams were pasted in as bare noun phrases and produced unreadable sentences: "The frame works as a user experience (UX) design RFP…" (vague subject) and "questions to ask in a design RFP interview test method" (garden path, read as "interview test method"). In the same post, "Fill the template in this order, starting with the grid" contradicted itself. Rule: give every secondary n-gram an article and a full verb ("the questions to ask in a design RFP interview should test…"), read each n-gram sentence aloud, and check that any "in this order / starting with" pointer names the step that really comes first.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp, QA loop 1): the handoff half of the acceptance section lived only in the image and its collapsed "Show as text" block, so Storybook had 0 open-text mentions and the breakpoints attribute had no explanation. QA counts collapsed-only entities as mentioned in passing.
