## Owner rules (highest priority — written by the site owner, never removed by agents)

<!-- add owner rules here, e.g. "- Always prefer US cost/pricing keywords for the SaaS silo." -->
- 2026-10-06 (owner): Check semantic completeness, not only correctness. Any incomplete blog, section, paragraph or headline (thin H2, missing brief point/entity, trailing sentence, thin FAQ, no next step) is a FAIL, and you must guide the writer: list every gap under "## Guidance for the writer" with where, what is missing, what to add and target length.
- 2026-10-06 (owner, REPLACES the 2026-10-05 duplication rule): An image may repeat a table/list ONLY if that table/list is wrapped in `<details class="astext">` (collapsed "Show as text"). FAIL if the same content is visible twice in the open, or if an image drops or changes items of the block it represents.
- 2026-10-05 (owner): Image/table duplication is a FAIL. For every image, read the 10 lines around it; if a table or list nearby carries the same items, FAIL it and name the image. I missed this in choose-ux-research-agency (4 images repeated their tables/lists) and the owner caught it after publishing.
- 2026-10-05 (owner): FAIL a bold H2 answer longer than ~30 words (one sentence). Report the H2 and a shorter version.
- 2026-10-08 (escalated by onpage-seo-auditor, repeat gap, not an owner rule; owner may edit): FAIL a `description` whose last sentence or clause is not a reader next step. Evidence: design-system-roi passed QA ("147 chars … reads naturally") with a description that ends on its contents list, as do choose-ux-research-agency and saas-product-redesign. How to measure: quote the description and name the next-step clause. It must be a verb the reader does ("request a fixed-scope proposal", "book a free consultation", "download the checklist") that matches the funnel CTA. Measure 140–158 chars on the built HTML. Good: "… one-page CFO business case. Then request a fixed-scope proposal." Bad: "… a one-page business case your CFO can approve."
- 2026-10-08 (escalated by onpage-seo-auditor, repeat gap, not an owner rule): the 2026-10-07 keyword-sentence check missed a second grammar defect in a keyword sentence. "Below is how to calculate design system ROI step by step, priced against…" passed QA in 2 loops, but "priced" dangles. For every quoted keyword sentence, also check that each participle after a comma describes the noun right before it. Run `grep -nE '^(\*\*)?[0-9]+ ' content/blog/<slug>.md` to catch sentences and bold answers that open with a digit ("**4 metrics prove…**"). FAIL either defect and give the rewritten sentence.
- 2026-10-09 (escalated by onpage-seo-auditor, repeat gap, not an owner rule; blog AND location pages): FAIL a keyword stacked as a modifier onto another noun. Evidence: 10-07 "settle UX audit agency vs freelancer", and 10-09 Philadelphia "How much does a UX design agency Philadelphia project cost?" (H2 + bold answer). Both scored 100. How to measure: grep the page for the keyword followed directly by a noun (`grep -niE '<keyword> (project|engagement|team|cost|pricing)'`) and run the "this agency" swap on each hit. Chicago's "What does a UX design agency Chicago engagement cost?" fails the same test. Good: "a Philadelphia UX design project". Bad: "a UX design agency Philadelphia project". Update 2026-10-10: it happened again on Columbus ("Our UX design agency Columbus work", "A UX design agency Columbus engagement"). It also happened with a brief secondary n-gram used as a bare subject in is-discovery-phase-worth-it ("so discovery phase vs MVP is a false choice"). Loop 1 QA noted the MVP expansion in that sentence but not its grammar. Also run `grep -niE '[^ ]+ vs [^ ]+' content/blog/<slug>.md` and FAIL any hit that lacks a determiner or carrier ("the X vs Y question", "framing it as X vs Y"). Update 2026-10-11 (repeat): QA passed "Weighing a mobile app design agency vs freelancer usually ends…" (mobile-app-design-agency-vs-freelancer closing) because it has a determiner. The determiner belongs only to the first half. How to measure: for every `vs` hit, quote it in the report and mark (a) is there a head noun after the pair (question/decision/choice)? or (b) if not, does each half carry its own article? FAIL when both answers are no. The "this choice" swap cannot catch this case. For location pages, also grep `'<keyword> [a-z]+'` in the JSON (Austin shipped "Our UX design agency Austin practice").
- 2026-10-10 (escalated by onpage-seo-auditor, repeat gap in one post, not an owner rule): FAIL any "every / always / all / never" claim that contradicts the post's own decision rule, wherever it sits, including text pasted from the fact-checker. Evidence: in is-discovery-phase-worth-it, loop 1 caught the closing ("the first step of every engagement"), but the intro kept "the first two of five steps in every engagement" (facts.md L49 replacement text) above a skip test that says 5–6 yes answers skip discovery. The post shipped with it. How to measure: `grep -niE '\b(every|always|all|never)\b' content/blog/<slug>.md`, quote each hit in the report and test it against the decision rule or thresholds. Good: "the first two of the engagement's five steps". Bad: "the first two of five steps in every engagement".

- 2026-10-12 (escalated by onpage-seo-auditor, repeat gap, not an owner rule; owner may edit): before PASS on publish day, list every `backlinkSuggestions` entry in the brief with its status: applied (quote the live anchor and `href`), skipped with a reason, or conditional (sent to the owner). A suggestion marked "optional" or "lower priority" with no status is a FAIL, not a skip. Evidence: 10-11 the optional home FAQ link to mobile-app-design-agency-vs-freelancer was missing at publish. 10-12 the optional UX research hub link ("hard-to-recruit participants, for example hospital clinicians" → usability-testing-cost-per-participant#what-does-recruiting-add-to-each-participant) was missing, while the required hub link was live both times. The auditor added both afterwards. How to measure: `grep -rln "/blog/<slug>/" content/ | grep -v briefs` must list every suggested source file, unless a reason is written in the QA report.
- 2026-10-12 (owner): run `npm run onpage -- /blog/<slug>/` and fail the post if `keyword-volume`, `meta-keywords-keyword`, `og-keyword`, `entity-coverage`, `attribute-coverage` or `entity-salience` (< 75) fails. Also fail any entity that is only name-dropped without a fact or explanation.
- 2026-10-12 (owner, layout-aware pages): fail the post if `npm run onpage -- /blog/<slug>/` reports `no-hidden-content`, `figure-caption`, `h2-then-text` or `table-intro`. Also check by eye that each image sits right after the paragraph it explains.
- 2026-10-14 (owner, concept bindings suitability check, RULES.md "Concept bindings" rule 7): FAIL a new blog, industry or location page when any brief `bindings.synonyms` entry, the parent or an `essential` concept is missing from the page; when an H2's first sentence lacks the central entity/synonym plus one essential concept; when a composite concept is split or reordered; when an `ambiguous` word appears without its context word in the same sentence; or when a `queryVariants` entry is not answered. List each failure with the line and the fix.
- 2026-10-20 (owner): blog infographics follow docs/content-system/visual-style/README.md (realistic mockups, embedded Manrope, scan-first type, 8-point spacing measured to the pixel). QA no longer fails images for text under 38px; it fails them for fallback fonts, placeholder-only UI, uneven gaps (run measure-cards.cjs or an equivalent getBBox check) or panel items missing from the image.

## Lessons (learned from runs, QA, audits and results)

<!-- lessons below -->
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency): the SVGs looked fine as native-size screenshots, but inside the built page (`.prose img`, 628px desktop / 354px mobile) the 14–18px text rendered at 4–9px. Images on the page are `loading="lazy"`, so `naturalWidth` reads 0 until each image is scrolled into view. Rule: serve `out/` locally (python http.server), scroll each image into view, record rendered width ÷ viewBox width, and judge legibility at rendered text size (≥ 12px desktop). Do not judge from native-size renders.
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency): reading the built FAQPage JSON-LD showed that a closing paragraph after the last FAQ was included in that FAQ's schema answer. The markdown alone did not show this. Rule: always print each FAQPage `acceptedAnswer.text` from `out/…/index.html` and check that it holds only the answer.
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency): the `beats` page was EGRESS_BLOCKED, and a WebSearch of the exact title returned the page's opening definition and framing snippets, enough to judge intro and focus but not a 5-gram percentage. Rule: when the fetch is blocked, compare structure from the snippets, say in the report that the numeric overlap could not be computed, and still require every serpGap to be covered.
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency, round 2): round 1 passed the cover from desktop alone. At 390px the `.pcover` box switches to 16:9 with `object-fit: cover`, which cropped the 1600×600 cover's left-aligned title. Rule: for every image, read the container's computed `object-fit` and `aspect-ratio` at each viewport, then screenshot the element at 390px and 1440px. Inline images and the cover both count; a cropped hero is a FAIL even when the inline graphics pass.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign): the designer and writer both logged that they "fixed" the rollout image's duplication by switching to an exposure-over-time axis, yet a label-by-label check showed every label on the image already in the list or the sentence under it. Rule: for each image, list its text labels and tick each one off against the adjacent list or table and the following paragraph. If nearly all are present, FAIL it whatever the other agents' reports say. An axis change is not new content.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign): text, alt, spelling and legibility checks all passed on the decision tree, but walking each root-to-leaf path showed a branch that gave wrong advice and a footer that pointed to prices the table does not hold. Rule: for any decision tree or flow image, walk every path as a concrete case and compare it with the post's prose. Check every "see the table/section" pointer on an image against the real content.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign, round 2): the post no longer referenced the removed image, yet the file was still in `out/`. Deleting the image line had also glued an H2 to the paragraph above it, and `.visuals.json` still held the old tree logic. Rule: after any image removal or redraw, `ls out/blog/<slug>/` against the post's image list, check the blank line around each edited spot, and diff `.visuals.json` markers against the redrawn files.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign, round 2): `serve` on the requested port answered even though a leftover server (PID started in an earlier round) already held it, so a 200 after my `kill` came from the old process. Rule: before serving, run `ps -eo pid,cmd | grep serve` and record the PIDs that already exist. After stopping my own PID, confirm with `ps` rather than curl, and report any leftover server instead of killing it blindly.
- 2026-10-06 (onpage audit, choose-ux-research-agency): the QA report checked the description's length (151) but not whether it held the keyword, and the post went live at 96 on the on-page score with `desc-keyword` failing. Rule: after the build, run `npm run onpage -- /blog/<slug>/` and paste its `score` and `failed` list into the QA report. FAIL below 95, and FAIL any `title-keyword`, `desc-keyword` or `h1-keyword` miss, whatever the total. Measure lengths on the built HTML: entities such as `&#x27;` for an apostrophe add characters there.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer): every brief entity "appeared" in the post, but Hotjar and Microsoft Clarity appeared only inside collapsed `<details>` blocks, so the brief attribute "analytics and session-recording synthesis" had no substance in the open text. Rule: count entity mentions twice, in the full body and in the body with `<details>…</details>` stripped. An entity found only in collapsed text counts as "mentioned in passing", so it becomes a writer gap.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer): `nohup python3 -m http.server … & echo $!` printed the wrapper's PID (2071). `kill 2071` left the real server (2073) running, and only `ps` showed it. Rule: after starting a server, read its PID from `ps -eo pid,cmd | grep http.server`, not from `$!`, and confirm it is gone with `ps` after the kill.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer): `npm run seo:check` exited 1 because of 2 broken `/work/<slug>/` links on a published location page that this post does not touch (case studies live at `/case-studies/<slug>/`). Rule: when seo:check errors come from other pages, check `git status`/`git log` to show they are pre-existing. Report them as an orchestrator FIX with exact old → new strings and keep them apart from the post's own findings, but still list the gate as blocked.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer, loop 2): every loop-1 fix landed. The new defect came from a *repair*: the fact-checker's wording was copied into the bold answer, so the bold answer and the sentence under it became near-verbatim duplicates. Rule: in every repair loop, diff the post against the previous commit (`git diff <prev> -- content/blog/<slug>.md`) and reread each changed bold answer together with the sentence that follows it. Repairs create new repetitions that a check of the loop-1 items alone will not catch.
- 2026-10-07 (onpage audit, ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer): QA recorded `onpage` 100 and passed, but the first sentence read "Before you settle UX audit agency vs freelancer, weigh…". The keyword was there, but the sentence was ungrammatical. `npm run onpage` only checks whether the phrase is present, never how it reads. Rule: for every place the script checks (title, H1, description, first 100 words), quote the sentence that holds the keyword in the QA report and pass it only if it still reads naturally when the keyword is swapped for "this choice" or "this service". FAIL a keyword dropped in without an article or a head noun ("settle UX audit agency vs freelancer"), and give the fix ("settle the UX audit agency vs freelancer question").
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi): the funnel decision was not settled by the SERP alone. Switching to MOFU would have made the template (`app/blog/[slug]/page.tsx` L100–103) render "See how we did this for <cases[0]>" using any case study that matches the post's industry (TradeZella, a dashboard revamp), which implies a result we do not have. Rule: before moving a post to MOFU, check what `relatedCaseStudies(post)[0]` returns. If no case study used the hub service, MOFU produces a misleading CTA, so keep BOFU or flag the template.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi): every chart number and payback month matched the post, but recomputing the model from the post's own inputs exposed 2 omissions. The blended rate was $86.13, not the stated $85. The model also had no migration or adoption-support rows, though the next section says "most models leave them out". Rule: for an illustrative model, re-derive every assumption from the figures the post cites, and check that the model includes every cost the post itself warns about. Give any missing lever as an exact recomputed sentence the writer can paste.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi, loop 2): I checked every repaired sentence for grammar and repetition. The fact-checker still caught a logic error in the new closing that I would have passed: "the build fee is the one row your own data cannot fill" was false, because the post's own tooling row comes from vendor quotes (L75, L147). Rule: when a repair adds an exclusivity claim ("the one", "only", "never", "every"), test it against the post's own inputs table and lists before passing it.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi, loop 2): the designer's SVG repair was in the working tree but not in the repair commit, so the committed post (L109) and the committed chart disagreed. Rule: in every repair loop, run `git status -- public/blog/<slug>/ content/briefs/<slug>*` and list each uncommitted asset as an orchestrator housekeeping FIX. A build from the working tree hides the mismatch.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp): Playwright element screenshots in the built page captured the sticky header and the floating "Our services / Free consultation" bar over the image (section map and acceptance card). That looked like a rendering defect, but it was not one. The SHOW_DRAFTS build also rendered a "← Previous guide" link to a draft stub (fintech-ux-design). Rule: before judging image overlap, hide `position: fixed/sticky` elements (or scroll the image to the viewport center), and treat draft-only silo navigation as an orchestrator check on the production build, not a post defect. Playwright is global (`$(npm root -g)/playwright`) with Chromium at /opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1194.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp): an abbreviation (WCAG, Q&A) expanded only inside a collapsed `<details>` table passed a DOM-order check, yet its first open-text use was bare. Rule: check abbreviation expansion on the text with `<details>` stripped, the same way entity coverage is checked.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp, loop 2): to clear overlays I set `display:none` on every fixed/sticky element, and that removed the sticky sidebar from the `.pgrid3` grid. Inline images then measured 220px wide at 1440 instead of the real 760px, which would have meant a false legibility FAIL. Rule: hide overlays with `visibility: hidden` (the layout keeps its space). Then check the image's ancestor widths (`article.prose` should be 760 at 1440) against the previous loop's numbers before judging the scale.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp, loop 2): a fact-check repair removed "mirrors how an agency builds a quote", and the new sentence "The order runs from who uses the product…" skipped sections 1–2 of the nine it describes. Rule: when a repair rewrites a sentence that summarises a list (order, steps, sections), check that it still describes the list's first and last items.
- 2026-10-09 (onpage audit, web-app-design-rfp): the post passed 2 QA loops with the bold answer "when procurement rules, a public-sector buyer or three or more bidding agencies demand written proposals". The third subject cannot take the verb, because bidders write proposals and do not demand them. Rule: for every bold answer and description that lists 3 or more subjects or objects sharing one verb, quote each pair ("X demands …") in the report and FAIL any pair that is false or ungrammatical on its own. Give the rewrite.
- 2026-10-10 (is-discovery-phase-worth-it): on-page scored 100 and every keyword sentence passed, yet the closing "runs discovery as the first step of every … engagement" contradicted the post's own skip test (5–6 yes = skip discovery). An exclusivity-claim check limited to repaired sentences would have missed it. Rule: in loop 1 too, test every "every/always/all" claim in the closing and the takeaways against the post's decision rule or thresholds. Also check a researcher-prescribed description ("use as written") for pronoun antecedents. It is not exempt.
- 2026-10-10 (is-discovery-phase-worth-it): element screenshots at 1440 showed a pink line whose width grew with scroll position (45 → 230 → 445 → 710px). It was the header's reading-progress bar, which stays visible because a child can override `visibility: hidden` on its parent. The native SVG render had no line. Rule: when a stray line or shape appears in only some screenshots, render the SVG file natively before calling it an image defect.
- 2026-10-10 (is-discovery-phase-worth-it, loop 2): the production build (no SHOW_DRAFTS) still created `out/blog/<slug>/`, because `public/blog/<slug>/*.svg` is copied as-is. That folder looks like the draft leaking, but it is not. Rule: to prove a draft is not live, check for no `out/blog/<slug>/index.html`, no sitemap entry, and 0 hits for `grep -rl 'href="/blog/<slug>'` over `out/**/*.html`. Do not rely on the folder being absent. After the check, note that `out/` now holds the production build.
- 2026-10-10 (is-discovery-phase-worth-it, loop 2): I was about to FIX "Every project begins with a free consultation and a fixed-scope proposal" as a reused closing (4 consecutive posts). The writer learnings (2026-10-09) show the orchestrator sanctions it as about.md's once-only offer sentence. Rule: before flagging a repeated CTA sentence, grep the writer and fact-checker learnings for an orchestrator policy on it. If one exists, record it as an owner note instead of a FIX, and verify `free consultation` appears exactly once.
- 2026-10-11 (mobile-app-design-agency-vs-freelancer): 5-gram overlap with the same-frame post (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer) was 0.6%, yet takeaway 2, the intro verdict and the closing price sentence were paraphrases of it ("one platform, few flows, in-house owner"; "our X run $A–$B over N weeks; the hub explains [pricing]"; "smaller project, not a cheaper one" vs "the cheaper quote is often just a smaller audit"). Rule: n-gram scores miss paraphrase. For a same-frame post, set the takeaways, the intro's last 2 sentences, the price sentence and the closing side by side and FAIL matching shapes.
- 2026-10-11 (mobile-app-design-agency-vs-freelancer): the routing summaries contained no every/always words, yet they routed on "a few flows", a criterion the decision tree never asks. A non-superlative bold answer ("Handoff is where the two models differ most") also contradicted the post's own skill map. Rule: walk one counter-case from the tree through every routing summary (intro, takeaways, routing FAQ, stage note), and test each superlative ("most", "the main") against the post's own images and tables.
- 2026-10-11 (mobile-app-design-agency-vs-freelancer, loop 2): the orchestrator reported that the "routing summaries were rewritten in tree order". The intro, takeaway and FAQ were, but the routing H2's bold answer (accepted in loop 1 as "exact against the tree") still put the launch-date override last. The freelancer scenario bullets also routed two-platform cases to a freelancer, because their scope was not stated. Rule: the routing set includes the routing H2's bold answer and every scenario bullet or list header. Walk the override counter-case (fixed date plus an otherwise-freelancer app) and the shared-node counter-case (two platforms, nobody in-house) through each item, in loop 1 too.
- 2026-10-11 (mobile-app-design-agency-vs-freelancer, loop 2): a repair added `<!-- source: URL -->` after a sentence. Markdown passes it through, so it shipped in the public HTML and twice in the RSC payload. Rule: in every loop, run `grep -n '<!--' content/blog/<slug>.md` and grep the built HTML for the post's comments. FIX any left-over source, visual or link comment into a real citation or delete it.
- 2026-10-11 (mobile-app-design-agency-vs-freelancer, loop 2): my own G2 guidance ("set the capped total beside the proposal's included rounds") was pasted as written and compared a dollar total with a count of rounds. Rule: before writing a replacement or guidance sentence, run the shared-verb pair test on it ("total beside price", not "total beside rounds"), the same test applied to the writer's text.
- 2026-10-12 (usability-testing-cost-per-participant): the designer flagged near-repeats between bold answers and three images (receipt, donut, round strip). Ticking each label against the section's open prose split them. The receipt and donut add content the prose lacks (sourced ranges; per-segment tasks and the 1–4 scale). The strip added nothing beyond its bold answer, and its "Sessions: covered" state contradicted L32 elsewhere in the post. Rule: decide near-repeat flags image by image with the label tick-off. When an image repeats only the bold answer, rewrite the bold answer so it no longer enumerates the items, and keep the image. Then test every state an image draws (covered/not, yes/no, shading order) against the whole post, not only its section.
- 2026-10-12 (usability-testing-cost-per-participant): I almost prescribed a third hub link to reach 6 internal links. design-system-roi.qa.md had already accepted 5 for a new BOFU silo (orchestrator exception), and the link-builder spec is BOFU 5–8. Rule: before writing a links FIX, grep previous `content/briefs/*.qa.md` and `.claude/agents/content-link-builder.md` for the funnel's link range and any exception. Never fix the count by breaking the hub cap.
- 2026-10-12 (usability-testing-cost-per-participant, loop 2): every F/G fix landed, but the writer's new G1 paragraph said "price each one as your plan × share", then described a division. The only new defect in loop 2 again came from text added by the repair, not from old text. Rule: in the final loop, read each sentence added by the repair as a formula or claim on its own terms (does the operator match the arithmetic it describes?) before checking anything else.
- 2026-10-12 (usability-testing-cost-per-participant, loop 2): the orchestrator's "~2,560 words" counted the `<details>` text, while the open body was about 2,160. Rule: before trimming for length, count open text only (strip `<details>` and image alt) and report both numbers, so nobody cuts a substantive section to fix a counting artefact. Update 2026-10-20 (how-to-design-an-mvp): the same counter gave published ux-strategy-roadmap 2,471 open words and ecommerce-ux-audit 2,372 (a recorded 2,200, 3,328 raw tokens), so the 2,200 cap is not measured the same way across posts. Judge the cap by parity: run the same script on the new post and on the last 2 published posts of the same shape. Trim restatements, duplicate CTAs and FAQ padding, then stop once the post matches the published posts. Never cut a brief entity, attribute or serpGap to hit the number.
- 2026-10-12 (onpage audit, usability-testing-cost-per-participant): the post passed 2 QA loops and scored 100, yet 2 of its 3 "Why" H2s opened the bold answer with "Because…", a fragment that fails as a standalone snippet. How to measure: `awk '/^## Why/{h=$0;getline;getline;if($0 ~ /^\*\*Because/)print h}' content/blog/<slug>.md` must print nothing. For each hit, FAIL it and give the writer a rewrite that restates the subject in ≤ 30 words (bad "**Because you pay to recruit people who never join…**", good "**No-shows raise the cost of each session because you pay to recruit people who never join…**"). The same check applies to "How come" H2s and to FAQ answers under "Why" questions.
- 2026-10-13 (onpage audit, web-app-design-rfp and is-discovery-phase-worth-it): an `entity-coverage` or `attribute-coverage` fail does not by itself show that the content is thin. In the RFP post, 4 of the 6 "missing" entities are explained in other words ("permissions" 9×, "workflow inventory" 2×, "bulk actions" 3×, "Revised 508" 1×). The brief entries are compound strings that cannot match. Rule: for each missing item the script prints, grep the head noun (`grep -oi '<head noun>' content/blog/<slug>.md | wc -l`) in the open text, with `<details>` stripped. Then sort it into one of two buckets in the report. **Covered, other words**: give the writer one sentence to put the brief's exact noun in, or send a brief-format fix to the researcher when the entry has a list, a slash or brackets in the middle. **Not covered**: a writer gap with the fact to add. FAIL only on the "not covered" bucket plus any item still failing after the fix. Never pass a post because "the concept is there" without a line for each item.
- 2026-10-14 (ecommerce-ux-audit): n-gram overlap with the last BOFU post was 0.42%, yet the closing line ("Your X …; the Y …") and the CTA sentence ("[send us your A](/contact/) with B, so C") copied its shapes. That only showed when I printed the last 3 posts' closing and CTA lines side by side. Rule: in every round, print `awk '/^---/{c++;next} c>=2' content/blog/<prev>.md | grep -n '/contact/'` and the line after it for the last 3 BOFU posts, set them against the new post's CTA and closing lines, and FAIL a matching sentence shape even when overlap is near 0.
- 2026-10-14 (ecommerce-ux-audit): the brief's anti-stack rule ("avoid … quote/findings stacks") collided with its own outline H2s. Rewording them would have dropped keyword H2s from 5/9 to 3/9, below the 40% salience rule. Rule: before writing a FIX that removes a keyword from a heading, recount keyword H2s after the change. If the count falls below 40%, keep the heading, record the reason in the report and send a lesson to the researcher instead.
- 2026-10-14 (ecommerce-ux-audit, loop 2): every F/G/D fix landed, but the G1 gap fill (a new paragraph appended at the end of the checkout H2) became the section's last paragraph and ended on a diagnosis ("…flag a confirmation page that falls short"), so the section lost the reader next step it had before. Rule: when a G-instruction appends a paragraph at a section end, the instruction itself must say "end on a reader action".
