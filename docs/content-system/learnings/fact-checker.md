## Lessons (learned from runs, QA, audits and results)

- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency): The post cited the right NN/g URL but changed its numbers ("4–5 per group" where the article says 3–4 per group for two groups and 3 for three or more). Rule: when a sentence gives a number next to a correct citation, check every number against that source separately. A correct link does not prove the figure.
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency): The writer's "lead moment" offer (a 30-minute call with the lead researcher, the researcher on the line) came from the brief's `leadAngle`, not from the site. The site only promises "a free consultation and a fixed-scope proposal" (`content/pages/about.md`), and the team in `content/home.ts` has no researcher title. Rule: check every first-person offer (call length, who attends, fixed price, turnaround) against about.md, the form cards and home.ts. Treat the brief's leadAngle as unverified.
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency): A buyer rubric ("if the answer is 'our design team', research is a phase") contradicted the site's own positioning ("The designers who run your research…"), so readers who applied the post's rubric would mark the agency down. Rule: when a post gives a vendor-evaluation rubric, score our own agency against it using the site's claims, and flag any criterion we would fail.
- 2026-10-05 (method): WebFetch was blocked (EGRESS_BLOCKED) for insightsassociation.org and other domains. A WebSearch limited to the source's domain that returns the exact URL plus supporting snippets worked for verification. If the post's URL differs from the URL the search returns (for example, no `www.`), mark the link UNVERIFIABLE and give the URL the search returned as the replacement.
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency, round 2): The writer pasted my round-1 replacement sentences word for word, so any wording I added became a new claim. One example is "at least 3 per group", where the NN/g source says "3 users from each category" and supports "at least" only in a parenthetical. Rule: before proposing a replacement sentence, check its exact wording against the source as strictly as the original post. In repair rounds, re-check the pasted replacements rather than assuming they are TRUE because I wrote them.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign): The scope decision tree SVG sent "several lifecycle stages do not leak" to "Not a redesign: price, positioning or a missing integration". The post ties that verdict to what churn interviews say, so the diagram made a claim the text never made, and the alt text repeated it. Rule: walk every branch of a decision tree or matrix in the SVG and check each leaf against the post's own conditions, not only the labels. A branch the prose does not support is WRONG even when every label is spelled correctly.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign): The post's CTA merged the free consultation with the hub's paid discovery step ("the free consultation returns … a baseline-and-rollout plan … run as the discovery step"). It also turned the brief's "first-hand angle" (week-1 asks: ticket export, churn-survey text, top accounts by seat count) into "we ask for" statements that no site page makes. Rule: for every "we …" sentence, find which site step it belongs to (free consultation in about.md, or a numbered process step on the hub) and flag sentences that mix the two or add deliverables. When a "we" process claim is not on the site, rewrite it as advice to the reader (imperative) instead of deleting it.
- 2026-10-06 (method): library.gv.com search results carry a Medium `?gi=` tracking parameter. A difference of only a tracking query string is not a URL mismatch, unlike a host difference such as a missing `www.`. Rule: compare host and path. Ignore tracking parameters (`?gi=`, `utm_*`) when deciding whether the post's link matches the URL the search returned.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign, round 2): The writer pasted F2 and F3 word for word but rewrote F5 freely. The rewrite brought back an offer that is not on the site ("a fixed price inside our published ranges"), even though `terms.md` says the ranges are "not an offer". My own F5 also said discovery "sets the baseline", which the hub step does not say. Rule: build CTA replacements only from phrases copied word for word from about.md and the hub's process step, and say so in the fix. In repair rounds, diff every CTA sentence against the round-1 replacement, because a sentence that was not pasted is a new claim.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign, round 2): A redrawn SVG may phrase a node differently from my fix (for example "Can users show the cause is UX?" instead of "Do churn interviews point to the UX?"). Rule: judge a redrawn graphic against the post's own text, not against my suggested wording. Accept different wording when every branch still matches a condition the post states.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer): The post set two numbers side by side ("one evaluator 20–50%, 3–5 evaluators 74–87%"). They came from two different Nielsen studies with different evaluator types: Nielsen and Molich 1990 (non-specialists, "20 and 51%") and Nielsen 1992 (3–5 usability *specialists*). The takeaway turned the pair into a present-tense rule. Search summaries restricted to nngroup.com mixed in arXiv text, so I could not tie the figures to the linked NN/g page. Rule: when a post pairs two figures, trace each figure to its own primary study and check that the population qualifier ("specialists", "average") survives into every takeaway, bold answer and SVG label. Do not accept a domain-limited search summary as proof that a figure is on a page when the result list also includes other domains.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer): The post put "our UX audit services" in the agency column of a provider matrix. That made every agency cell a claim about us, including "keyboard and screen-reader passes", which no site page states. Separately, the hub says "2 designers" while home.ts says "1–2 designers". Rule: when a post places us in one column of a comparison, check every cell in that column against the hub. Before accepting any first-person headcount, grep `home.ts` and the hub for staffing counts. If they disagree, remove the count from the post and flag the inconsistency to the orchestrator.
- 2026-10-07 (method): Hertzum and Jacobsen's abstract gives "the *average* agreement between any 2 evaluators … ranges from 5% to 65%". The post left out "average", which changed the meaning. Rule: when a source reports a range of averages, the post must keep the word "average" (or "mean", "median"). A range without its statistic is WRONG, not a harmless paraphrase.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer, loop 2): When the writer changed "20–50%" to "20–51%" in the coverage-curve SVG, the y-axis tick had to move too. A label fix does not prove the plotted geometry moved with it. Rule: for any number fixed in a chart SVG, compute the expected coordinate from the axis endpoints (here 0% at y=640, 100% at y=250, so 3.9 px per %) and check that the tick, bar and band edges sit there. Don't stop at the text label.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer, loop 2): "None of [axe DevTools, WAVE, Lighthouse] can judge whether alt text makes sense" is true for their automated rules. However, Deque's paid axe DevTools Pro tier adds "Intelligent Guided Tests" that ask a person whether alt text describes the image. Rule: when a post says what a named tool cannot do, search the vendor's own domain for paid or guided tiers. Accept the claim only if it is scoped to automated checks or the paid feature still depends on human judgement, and note the caveat in the report.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi): The link-builder updated the BLS medians from May 2024 to May 2025, and the $92/$68 rates were updated with them. The worked example's "$85 blended (… BLS medians ÷ 0.70)" was not updated, and it now computes to $86. Because every chart point is priced in that rate, recomputing it would have forced a full redraw. Rule: when any source figure changes, trace every derived number that names its derivation, and recompute each one. For worked-example inputs, propose relabelling the input as a stated assumption (with the derived value shown next to it) before proposing a recompute, and list which results each option changes.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi): The post called Sparkbox's 8-developer Carbon test "the one public timing test with a stated method". A figma.com search found Figma's 2019 timed experiment (34% faster, method stated). Rule: for every "the only / the one / the first" claim about research, search for a counterexample, including vendor research on the tool maker's own blog, before marking it TRUE. Usually the fix is to change "the one" to "one".
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi): The post said "Chromatic visual tests catch coded components drifting from the Figma library". Chromatic's docs show that visual tests compare snapshots with the last approved baseline, and that Figma only links stories to components for review. Separately, "the UI inventory … gets its baseline" credited the engagement with all 3 baseline signals when it produces only one. Rule: for "tool/deliverable X catches or produces Y", check what X actually compares against or outputs, and narrow the claim to that object.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi, loop 2): The writer did not paste my loop-1 CTA. The rewritten closing kept the about.md offer word for word, but it added "the build fee is the one row your own data cannot fill". The post's own worksheet and cost list send the tooling line to vendor quotes, so the claim contradicted the post. Rule: "the one / the only" claims about the post's own model or worksheet also need a counterexample search, inside the post this time. Check them against every row and list in the post, not only against external sources.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi, loop 2): Two new sensitivity results (ramp to month 24 → payback month 32; +$34,000 migration → month 26) were quick to verify with a closed form for a linear ramp: S(m) = R·(m − F + (F − 3 + 1)/2) after full-rate month F, with cost C(m) = 127,500 + 6,000m. Rule: when a post's worked example gains sensitivity checks, write the closed form for cost and savings in the report. Check the claimed payback month and the month before it, so that rounding direction (ceiling of the crossing) is confirmed, and confirm that no earlier crossing exists during the ramp.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp): The post's 18F sentence ("review strengths, weaknesses and risks, invite the highest-rated to a verbal interview") was accurate. It sat directly before our own 100-point scorecard, but the same 18F guides say "Don't use a point system to score proposals." A true citation placed next to our advice can lend the source's authority to advice the source rejects. Rule: when a post cites an authority next to its own method (weights, scores, templates), search that source for what it says about that method. If the source contradicts the method, require a sentence that names the difference, not only a "suggestion" label.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp): Hub figures can be copied exactly and still be framed wrongly. "$60,000–$180,000 … driven by the same counts your inventory holds" used the right range, but the hub's 4 price factors include component library size, which the post's inventory never counts. The phrase "among the components a web app UI kit covers" likewise listed keyboard use and saved filters, which come from the hub's benefits card and not from its UI kit list. Rule: for every "because the hub says X" link, check which hub section the items come from (deliverable list, price factors or benefits) and whether the post's list is a true subset of that section.
- 2026-10-09 (method): WebFetch failed with `ENOTFOUND` for every host in this run, including w3.org and acquisition.gov. An exact-phrase WebSearch on the quote's tail (no domain filter) still returned the aiga.org page as the top hit, while the domain-limited summary only paraphrased it. Rule: to verify a quotation without WebFetch, run exact-phrase searches on the head and the tail separately and require the cited page to be a top result for each. Report punctuation inside the quote (dashes) as unverified rather than failing the claim.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp, loop 2): The writer adapted my F4 ("If yours stalls, …") into "If yours does, …". The sentence before it ends "no single team holds the full workflow list", so the elliptical "does" attaches to "holds" and reverses the condition. Rule: when a writer shortens a replacement with a pro-form ("does", "it", "this", "so"), read it together with the sentence before it and resolve the pro-form to the nearest verb or noun. If that reading changes the meaning, require the explicit verb.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp, loop 2): The new handoff paragraph took "three desktop breakpoints" from the hub FAQ (stated as fact) and the widths 1280/1440/1920 from the handoff card (stated "for example"). The post was right only because it kept "for example" on the widths. Rule: when a "we" sentence combines hub items from two sections, check each part's hedge in its own section, and require the post to keep "for example" on every part that the hub hedges.
- 2026-10-10 (is-discovery-phase-worth-it): Every case-study fact in the post was copied exactly, but the summary bold answer said early research "settled who the product serves". nitro.md's Overview gives the three audiences as the client's starting point, and Vocable's audience is stated in its challenge. Only X in Our approach or The solution was produced by the work. Build the replacement from approach/solution sentences only.
- 2026-10-10 (is-discovery-phase-worth-it): The single cross-hub link copied the startup hub's sprint deliverable correctly, then added "before you commit to the full design", which turned a step of a 4–8 week engagement into a buy-first gate. The post's FAQ also said a sprint "fits when [target users] are known", but the same startup hub says the sprint is what aligns founders on 1 target user. Rule: when a post links a sibling hub's deliverable, check every added clause about when or how it is bought against that hub's process steps. Then grep the post's FAQs for the same item and check that each mention agrees with that hub and with the post's own decision rules.
- 2026-10-10 (is-discovery-phase-worth-it, loop 2): The new closing promised to scope a proposal "whether your score points to full discovery, a light sprint or design right away". The hub FAQ says "Every digital product design engagement includes 5–8 user interviews per user group", so the "design right away" branch is an offer the site does not make. Rule: when a CTA promises to serve every outcome of the post's own decision tool (score bands, tree leaves), map each outcome to a site offer. Grep the hub FAQ for "every … includes" statements and fail any outcome that drops an included step.
- 2026-10-10 (is-discovery-phase-worth-it, loop 2): "In our digital product design services, discovery and user research are the first two steps; this post covers only whether to buy or skip them." The cost sentence in the same post was carefully neutral, but in this sentence "them" points to our own steps, which presents them as optional purchases. Rule: wherever a post names our engagement's steps, look in the same sentence and the next one for "buy / skip / add / optional / separately" and resolve each pronoun. If it lands on our steps, it is a "sold separately" claim, even when the price sentences are clean.
- 2026-10-10 (is-discovery-phase-worth-it, QA loop 2): my F1 replacement bold answer was pasted verbatim at 38 words, over the ~30-word owner cap. It listed three "gave X its Y" facts, then tacked on ", and the design was built on that", a second "and" clause the case lines below already carry. QA cut it to 31 words without touching a fact. Rule: when your fix replaces a bold H2 answer, keep it to one sentence of about 30 words or fewer, with no clause after a list's final "and". Put the extra point in the sentence under the bold. Also reread a replacement FAQ answer for a word you repeat ("tests it quickly … needs testing quickly").
- 2026-10-11 (mobile-app-design-agency-vs-freelancer): The decision tree SVG was correct on every branch, and so was the H2 bold answer. Run each one through the tree as a test input, including the Q1 override, and fail any that lands on a different leaf.
- 2026-10-11 (mobile-app-design-agency-vs-freelancer): The brief told the writer to say "several designers working in parallel" instead of a count. home.ts gives "2–4 designers" for the same price band, and the hub gives no number, so "several" (3 or more) is still a headcount claim. - 2026-10-11 (method): An iOS platform pair labelled "Wheel date picker" described the pre-iOS 14 default. - 2026-10-11 (mobile-app-design-agency-vs-freelancer, loop 2): The new illustrative line "with no one moderating tests, users meet the new onboarding flow only in the first TestFlight beta build" had no statistics, but its logic failed. - 2026-10-12 (usability-testing-cost-per-participant): The heat table had no numbers in its quote-from-recruiter cells, but its "darker = costs more" legend, "higher again" and "the highest on this table" ranked incentives for 5 audiences. - 2026-10-12 (usability-testing-cost-per-participant): "Specialist audiences are part of that work: in the TradeZella…" quoted the case study exactly, but "that work" meant the usability testing engagement, and tradezella.md's `services` list has no usability-testing-services. - 2026-10-12 (method, round 2): The only file tools available were Read and whole-file Write, while QA edited the post in parallel.
